Doing What Comes Naturally

What effort can teach us about our strengths

Some things become so familiar that we barely notice the skill involved. In this short experiment, you’ll complete a very familiar task twice and pay attention to what changes—not just in your performance, but in how much concentration and effort the task requires.

This is not a typing test. There is no “good” score, and speed is not the goal. You can also skip the activity and use a reflection-only version.

Compare the evidence

Same person. Same name. Same goal. Different pathway.

You did not suddenly lose your ability to spell your name. What changed was how naturally and fluently you could access what you already knew.

Now connect the experience to strengths

Some patterns of thinking, relating, deciding, organizing, creating, and responding come more naturally to us. We may have used them so often that we underestimate how much capability is actually there.

Other ways of working may still be possible for us—but they can require more deliberate attention, practice, and energy.

Very weak → adequate
Sometimes necessary.
OR
Good → great
Often overlooked.

Development does not only mean fixing weaknesses. Sometimes the greatest opportunity is investing deliberately in something that already comes naturally.

One more clue

What feels ordinary to you may be extraordinary to someone else.

Reflection-only pathway

Think of something that has become second nature

Think about an activity you can now do almost automatically—using software you know well, navigating a familiar place, preparing something you make often, playing an instrument, or another familiar activity.

Now imagine completing the same goal through an unfamiliar process. You could probably still do it—but it might require more attention, effort, and time. That contrast is the idea this activity is designed to surface.

Connect that contrast to strengths

Strengths can feel similar: patterns we can access readily, use repeatedly, and develop further. The goal is not to ignore weaker areas. It is to recognize that development can also mean investing in what already works well.

What feels ordinary to you may be extraordinary to someone else.
